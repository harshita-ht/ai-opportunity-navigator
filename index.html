Find where AI can actually move the needle.

Spotted a slow process, a costly error, or an opportunity to work better? Describe it in a few minutes. Every opportunity follows the same transparent review process.

The path of an opportunity

1
Submit an opportunity

Describe the problem, scale, proposed AI role, and initial risks.

2
Screening

An AI program team reviewer takes ownership, checks that the evidence is complete, and looks for duplicate opportunities.

3
Priority assessment

The reviewer scores business impact, technical feasibility, data readiness, and adoption readiness.

4
Recommendation

The reviewer recommends a pilot, further discovery, more information, a non-AI solution, or deferral.

5
Governance and pilot decision

Risk specialists join when needed before a separate decision is made about funding and launching a controlled pilot.

How opportunities are prioritized

The assigned reviewer uses the same four-part rubric for every opportunity. The weighted result is shown out of 10.

35%

Business impact

Time saved, cost reduced, revenue enabled, quality improved, or risk avoided—supported by the scale of the problem.

25%

Technical feasibility

Whether current AI tools can help reliably and how difficult the systems, integrations, and controls would be.

20%

Data readiness

Whether the required information exists, is accessible, has sufficient quality, and can be used appropriately.

20%

Adoption readiness

Whether there is a clear owner, user support, a workable human role, and a realistic path to changing the process.

Before you submit

✓
Name the problem, not the tool. Explain what is slow, costly, repetitive, or error-prone today.
✓
Give a rough sense of scale. Monthly cases, people involved, and time per case are enough to start.
✓
Say where the information lives. System and document names help the reviewer understand feasibility.
✓
Do not include sensitive records. Describe the data involved without pasting confidential content.
